Expected file format

The wide layout written by DataFrame.to_csv / to_parquet for a frame with a 3-level column index and a multi-level row index:

  • Columns (three header rows): date, fly_num, roi. Each column must be unique. roi is either a glomerulus name (D, DA1, … VM7v) or a non-negative integer ROI id. It may be called cells instead: its values are then KC ids, so must be non-negative integers (and the coding unit is taken to be KCs).
  • Rows (leading columns): at least odor1 (or odor); optionally panel, is_pair, odor2, …, repeat. Extra odor<n> levels are mixture components presented together in air.
  • Odor strings look like va @ -3: abbreviation, a space, @, a space, log10 concentration (0 or negative). solvent marks an unused component. A mixture may also be written in one level, its components joined by + (ea @ -4.2 + eb @ -3.5).

A long layout (one row per value, with columns date, fly_num, roi, [panel,] odor1, …, [repeat,] value) is also accepted; parquet files with integer fly_num usually need this layout.

For now, with a warning: no flies, columns of ROIs alone (in a CSV, a single header line of the row levels' names then the ROIs', as DataFrame.to_csv writes a frame with plain columns; in a long file, neither date nor fly_num), and no trials (no repeat level: each row is taken as one trial, as when already averaged over them). A _t0, _t1, … suffix is removed from ROI names (DA1_t0 → DA1), and an ROI of glomeruli that could not be told apart may be named by them joined with / (VA1d/VA1v).

Group the datasets by

A dataset's power range is its power ± the tolerance entered at upload, or ± 0.5 mW where none was entered. Two datasets can be in a group when their ranges overlap, or are at most this far apart (every two in a group).

A group's scale factor best matches (least squares, through zero) its mean responses to the odors it shares with the target to the target's, per glomerulus when all ROIs are glomeruli. A dataset's own concentration of an odor is used when it has it; otherwise its nearest one less than the distance above away (so with 1, x @ -3.9 stands for x @ -3, but x @ -4 does not). The odors in the download keep their own concentrations.

Between similarities

Of the datasets shown and the spaces compared to: over the pairs of odors both have, from one side of each similarity matrix (not its diagonal).

Each correlation's p-value (to the right of it) is two-sided, against no correlation. By bootstrap: twice the share of correlations, of what is resampled with replacement, on the other side of 0 (at least 2 / (resamplings + 1)). By permutation: the share of correlations, with one space's odors shuffled, as far from 0 or further (at least 1 / (permutations + 1)). As every two spaces shown are tested at once, these are adjusted by the Holm–Bonferroni method (Holm 1979): the chance of any false positive among them stays under 5% at p < 0.05 however the tests depend on each other, as these do, sharing spaces and odors; and it never finds fewer than Bonferroni's correction.

Pairs of odors are not independent: each odor is in many of them, so if odors differ in how strongly they respond (to anything), the pairs with a strong one move together. Bootstrapping pairs ignores this, so its p-values can be far too small: for unrelated spaces of such odors simulated here, p < 0.05 for a third to half of them, not 5%. Bootstrapping odors instead (and the pairs of those drawn) keeps that dependence, but its p-values are often too large. Permuting odors (a Mantel test; the stimulus-label randomization test of Nili et al. 2014) keeps it too, and is exact: for unrelated spaces, p < 0.05 at most 5% of the time, however their odors respond. Compared on glomeruli, the same goes for glomeruli in place of odors.

For more details, and the simulations behind them, see Statistical testing in the design notes.

Headspace calculator

How much of each odor of a mixture is in the air above it: odors of the odor table diluted together in one vial, at equilibrium at 25 °C. What it needs of each odor (molar mass, density, vapor pressure and, in water, Henry's law constant) is looked up through chemutils the first time, and kept with the odor table.

Mixture

    How it is computed
    • In paraffin oil, by Raoult's law for an ideal solution: each odor's partial pressure is its mole fraction in the liquid times its vapor pressure. Its moles are from its volume (the log10 v/v concentration), density and molar mass; the oil's as if it were n-hexadecane (226 g/mol, 0.770 g/mL). Polar odors (alcohols, acids) are less soluble in oil than an ideal solution, so their real headspace is likely higher when dilute.
    • In water, by Henry's law: each odor's partial pressure is its concentration in the water over its Henry's law solubility constant (from henrys-law.org, else NIST), but at most its vapor pressure. With no constant found, as an ideal solution, as in oil, which is likely too low unless it mixes well with water.
    • Vapor pressures are from PubChem (the mean of its experimental values at or near 25 °C), else estimated from the structure (SIMPOL.1, often within 3×, but several times too low for e.g. furans, tertiary alcohols and some phenols). With no density found, 1 g/mL is assumed.
    • Concentrations in the air are at 1 atm and 25 °C: ppm and ppb as parts of the air by volume (moles), mol/L and molecules/cm³ by the ideal gas law. At equilibrium, in a closed vial: air flowing over the liquid, and diluting it after, lowers what reaches the fly.

    For more details, see Headspace calculator in the design notes.

    Admin

    Settings for the whole site: which folders on the server this app may read and write (fixed when the server starts, so changes take effect when it restarts, which can be done below), the reference data it shows, deleting datasets, and the odor sheet it syncs the odor table from.

    Folders on the server

    Absolute paths on the server. Besides these, the app reads its own code and the system's, and reads and writes its data folder (…), where it keeps everything it stores; it writes nowhere else.

    May read

    Users' folders on tensor (…) can only be used where they are in one of these. A file alone may be listed too: a reference dataset added below adds its file here when it is outside these.

    May also write

    These can be read too. Backups (below) are made into one of them.

    Its own files

    Each file the server has read or written in the folder it runs from (…): its database, uploads and the rest of its data folder. Not listed: the repository's own files (its code and configuration), and those in the folders above or of reference data. Struck through: deleted since.

    Backups

    Copies everything the site keeps into a folder it may write (above, under May also write): its database, and each dataset's data file as uploaded, odor YAMLs, Experiment.xml, README and metadata.json, with the reference data it read in, the datasets deleted here and odor structure images. Not the raw and motion-corrected data, which stay on tensor, nor uploads still under way. Each backup is a folder olfdb-backup-<date>_<time> laid out like the data folder: to restore one, stop the server and put its contents into the data folder. Files unchanged since the backup before it in that folder are hard links to the copies there, so each backup takes only the space of what changed.

    Reference data

    Tables shown in the Reference data tab, each a CSV, TSV or parquet file on the server, which the app never changes: it reads it into its data folder, and again whenever it changes. Deleting one deletes only that copy. The file has row labels in its first column, an optional group column, then one column of numbers per matrix column (blank: missing). Its chemicals are shown by their names in the Odors tab, matched by CID (a column such as cid or pubchem_cid), else InChI (inchi), else CAS, MetaCyc or LOTUS ID, name or abbreviation (columns of these, or the labels themselves). When its columns are the chemicals, give these as rows instead (labelled cid, ...). Or an xarray DataArray of two dimensions saved as netCDF (.nc, by DataArray.to_netcdf): rows its first dimension, missing values taken as 0, each item labelled by its coordinate named name (or e.g. product_name), and those labelled alike told apart by its other coordinates that differ among them (all of them shown on hover); coordinates named as the columns above (cid, inchi, ...) identify chemicals.

    Odor names

    Refuses uploads whose odors (as named in their data) have capital letters, and odor sheet rows whose name, abbreviation or aliases do, unless the name is already in the database (in the odor table, or in a dataset). Anyone can also have odor names shown in lowercase, in their Settings.

    Odor properties from chemutils

    Each odor's vapor pressure and molar mass, and what the Headspace calculator needs of the odors used in it, are looked up through chemutils once, and kept: in the database, and in chemutils' own cache in the data folder. A newer chemutils (installed when the server is rebuilt with a newer one pinned in requirements.txt) does not change them, so where it changed how it looks something up, those looked up with an older one are counted below. Look them up again here: about a second each, not from chemutils' cache, each kept as it comes.

    Restart

    It takes a few seconds; uploads and downloads under way are given up to 30 s to finish.
